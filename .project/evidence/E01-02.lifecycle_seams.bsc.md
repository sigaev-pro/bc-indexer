## Fixture inputs

The genesis is the accepted private BSC genesis of the ProfileManifest
`local-runner-04-f2b351e/manifests/bsc.json` (`6c4e146a…`). The sealing key
is the public test keystore of the BSC source, as in the decision bridge and
corpus blocks. The probe generates its nine blocks with native Parlia; every
timestamp is fixed by its parent, so the blocks are the same in every run.
The driver checks each header (Keccak-256 hash, parent, number, time,
difficulty and validator) against that genesis.

One step uses a fixture: after the BOOTSTRAP rewind deleted S3, the probe
rewrites S3's header, body and total difficulty, as a snap-sync rollback
leaves a stored block above the head. The state of S3 is the native state
that the rewind kept. README "Limits" explains why the native callers alone
cannot leave such a block.

## Limits

The README "Limits" section explains each of them:
- the two rows deferred under D104 (E05-02.reorg owns the targeted replay);
- the known-block fixture step;
- the finalized clear of a rewind and the FINALITY step of the startup
  reconciliation are implemented but not exercised: without vote
  attestations, the finalized block stays genesis;
- the node wiring is proven in-process through `eth.New` and its RPC APIs,
  not through the shipped `geth` command line (E01-01.builds, E06);
- the probe calls `GoLive` where the live service would, because a node
  without peers never reports `Synced`;
- with no mirror (E05-01.registry), every decision stays an obligation, so
  the manual database guard refuses every capture store that has made a
  decision;
- the passes of `insertSideChain` are never entered by the probe.
